    21→    21→## 1. Overview
    22→    22→
    23→    23→**Haqqi** (working name — the brand can be finalized independently of this spec; nothing below depends on the final name) is a bilingual (Arabic-first, English-second) AI-assisted platform that helps car-accident victims in Jordan understand their likely legal entitlements, follow a structured claims timeline, organize evidence, recognize bad-faith insurer conduct, generate lawyer-reviewed draft documents (complaint letters, demand letters, court filings), and connect with a vetted lawyer.
    24→    24→
    25→    25→**Vision**: No one in Jordan should lose rightful compensation after a car accident due to lack of knowledge, fear, or an unfair claims process.
    26→    26→
    27→    27→**Positioning**: a self-help and case-organization tool with a built-in lawyer-review layer — not a substitute for legal representation, and every document that leaves the platform is either explicitly informational or has passed through the Lawyer Review workflow in Section 7.

    31→    31→## 2. Problem Statement
    32→    32→
    33→    33→| Pain Category | Description |
    34→    34→| --- | --- |
    35→    35→| **Information Asymmetry** | Victims don't know their entitlements (medical, disability, death, moral damages), deadlines, or required documents. |
    36→    36→| **Abusive Claims Handling** | Insurers may delay, deny, lowball, or misstate policy terms. |
    37→    37→| **Intimidation & Power Imbalance** | Aggressive adjusters or informal actors pressure victims into fast, unfair settlements. |
    38→    38→| **Informal Claim-Buying** | Third parties buy accident claims at a discount and cash out the difference. |
    39→    39→| **Weak Recourse Awareness** | A complaint path exists (insurer → CBJ → courts) but is not widely known or easy to use. |
    40→    40→| **Complex Court Procedures** | Victims don't know how to file a claim or enforce a judgment. |
    41→    41→
    42→    42→**Current state**: Jordan has compulsory third-party motor insurance, a Motor Accidents Compensation Fund for uninsured/hit-and-run cases, an insurance-sector consumer-protection function at CBJ, and a civil procedure code governing court claims. **All specific article/regulation numbers referenced anywhere in this document are placeholders pending confirmation by your engaged legal counsel** — see Section 7.4.

    73→    73→## 5. Scope & Features
    74→    74→
    75→    75→### 5.1 Phase 1 — Core Self-Help (informational only; nothing generated here is sent externally)
    76→    76→
    77→    77→#### 5.1.1 Rights & Entitlements Calculator
    78→    78→**Functional requirements**
    79→    79→* Guided Q&A (Arabic default, English toggle): accident type, injuries, death/disability, medical bills, lost income, vehicle damage, other party's insurance status.
    80→    80→* Output: compensation categories with plain-language legal basis (from the versioned, lawyer-approved rules config — Section 7.2), an estimated JD range with a "not legal advice" disclaimer, and a document checklist per category.
    81→    81→* Persist results to the user's profile if logged in.
    82→    82→
    83→    83→**Technical requirements**
    84→    84→* Next.js (App Router) + TypeScript. Compensation logic reads from a versioned JSON/YAML config table (`legal_rules_config`, Section 6.2), never hardcoded in application code, so legal counsel can update it via the Legal Content Management UI (Section 7.2) without a deploy.
    85→    85→
    86→    86→#### 5.1.2 Step-by-Step Accident Workflow
    87→    87→**Functional requirements**
    88→    88→* Auto-generated task timeline (police report, croquis, photos, medical exam, insurer notification, document submission, follow-up), driven by the same `legal_rules_config` as 5.1.1.
    89→    89→* Reminders via SMS/WhatsApp/email at key milestones. Progress tracker.
    90→    90→
    91→    91→**Technical requirements**
    92→    92→* Supabase Edge Functions (or Vercel Cron) for scheduled reminder jobs. Messaging sent through the provider-agnostic notification layer (Section 6.6).
    93→    93→
    94→    94→#### 5.1.3 Complaints Directory & Templates
    95→    95→**Functional requirements**
    96→    96→* Directory of insurer complaint contacts; CBJ contact details.
    97→    97→* Insurer- and CBJ-complaint templates auto-filled from case data. Export as PDF (Arabic RTL), email, or copy-to-clipboard.
    98→    98→
    99→    99→**Technical requirements**
   100→   100→* RTL-capable PDF generation (e.g., `@react-pdf/renderer` with Noto Sans Arabic embedded — validate in Section 9). Templates stored as MDX in the `legal_templates` table (Section 6.2), version-controlled and lawyer-approved before use (Section 7.2).
   101→   101→
   102→   102→#### 5.1.4 Anonymous Story & Abuse Reporting
   103→   103→**Functional requirements**
   104→   104→* Submission form, no PII required. Admin moderation queue before publishing. Public, filterable, anonymized stories page.
   105→   105→
   106→   106→**Technical requirements**
   107→   107→* Supabase RLS restricting raw submissions to admins. Rate limiting at the edge (e.g., Upstash) to prevent spam.
   108→   108→
   109→   109→#### 5.1.5 AI-Powered Step-by-Step Intake
   110→   110→**Functional requirements — 7 stages**
   111→   111→1. Triage & safety (death/injury/threats → emergency guidance).
   112→   112→2. Accident facts (date/time/location, police report, croquis, other party, insurance status).
   113→   113→3. Losses & damages.
   114→   114→4. Claims history (insurer, claim number, offers/denials).
   115→   115→5. Goals & constraints (desired outcome, lawyer budget, litigation willingness).
   116→   116→6. Document collection checklist.
   117→   117→7. Consent & disclaimers (active confirmation, not a pre-checked box).
   118→   118→* Output: structured `case_intake` JSON feeding the Calculator, Workflow, and (Phase 2) Drafting Mode.
   119→   119→
   120→   120→**Technical requirements**
   121→   121→* LLM provider abstracted behind a single interface (Section 6.4) so the underlying model (Grok, Gemini, or other) is swappable without touching intake logic — validate provider choice per Section 9 before committing.
   122→   122→* Structured output validated against a Zod/Pydantic schema before being trusted downstream. System prompt requires one question at a time, empathetic tone, and an explicit "I'm not sure — here's how to reach a lawyer" fallback rather than guessing.
   123→   123→
   124→   124→### 5.2 Phase 2 — AI Drafting & Case Management
   125→   125→
   126→   126→#### 5.2.1 Drafting Mode (AI Legal Companion)
   127→   127→**Functional requirements**
   128→   128→* Template library: insurer demand letter, CBJ complaint, statement of claim, settlement/release agreement, limited power of attorney, evidence list/chronology, expert request.
   129→   129→* Citation-aware drafting: each paragraph can show a "why this wording" panel citing the specific retrieved article.
   130→   130→* Plain-Arabic / Legal-Arabic toggle. Full version history.
   131→   131→* **Every draft must pass the Lawyer Review workflow (Section 7.1) before export/send is enabled** — this is enforced at the data layer, not just a UI checklist.
   132→   132→
   133→   133→**Technical requirements**
   134→   134→* Draft generation ≤ 60s after intake completion. Every legal claim in a draft carries at least one citation reference from the RAG layer. Full audit log of generation/edit/review/send events.
   135→   135→
   136→   136→#### 5.2.2 RAG Knowledge Base (Insurance Law & Court Procedure)
   137→   137→**Functional requirements**
   138→   138→* Indexed sources (priority order, each gated by `lawyer_verified = true` before indexing — Section 7.2): Civil Code liability/limitation provisions; compulsory motor insurance regulations; Civil Procedure Code; Motor Accidents Compensation Fund rules; CBJ Financial Consumer Protection instructions; publicly available Court of Cassation summaries for recurring ambiguities.
   139→   139→* Chunking by article/clause (200–400 tokens) with `{source, article_id, topic, language}` metadata. Hybrid retrieval (keyword + vector). Below-threshold retrieval confidence → assistant states uncertainty and offers lawyer referral instead of guessing.
   140→   140→
   141→   141→**Technical requirements**
   142→   142→* Supabase pgvector, 768-dim embeddings, ivfflat index. Retrieval latency ≤ 500ms. Weekly re-index or on-demand when a source changes.
   143→   143→
   144→   144→#### 5.2.3 Evidence Organizer & Claim Log
   145→   145→**Functional requirements**
   146→   146→* Uploads: police report, croquis, photos, medical reports, bills, salary slips, correspondence. Structured interaction log (date, contact, summary, outcome).
   147→   147→* Bad-faith pattern flags (repeated delays, unnecessary requests, lowball offers vs. calculated range). One-click demand-letter generation from the log (routes through 5.2.1 → 7.1).
   148→   148→
   149→   149→**Technical requirements**
   150→   150→* Supabase Storage with signed URLs. Malware scanning on upload. AES-256 at rest, TLS 1.3 in transit. File limits configurable, not hardcoded (validate real-world medical-file sizes in Section 9).
   151→   151→
   152→   152→#### 5.2.4 Lawyer & Legal Aid Directory
   153→   153→**Functional requirements**
   154→   154→* Filters: location, language, fee model, expertise. Profiles with bio, contact, verified-user reviews, self-reported success rate.
   155→   155→* One-click handoff packet: shares case intake, documents, claim log, and lawyer-approved drafts.
   156→   156→
   157→   157→**Technical requirements**
   158→   158→* Manual admin verification before a lawyer profile is live. Reviews restricted to verified accounts.
   159→   159→
   160→   160→#### 5.2.5 Anti-Corruption Education & Reporting
   161→   161→**Functional requirements**
   162→   162→* Education module on risks of selling/transferring a claim. Anonymous reporting form for suspicious actors, admin-only aggregation.
   163→   163→
   164→   164→**Technical requirements**
   165→   165→* No PII stored unless opted in. Encrypted at rest, admin-only access via RLS.
   166→   166→
   167→   167→### 5.3 Phase 3 — Litigation Support & Ecosystem
   168→   168→
   169→   169→#### 5.3.1 Court Procedure Guidance Module
   170→   170→Pre-filing checklist, statement-of-claim drafting, service/notification guidance, expert-evidence pathway, enforcement guidance — each step showing deadline, required form, fee, and a "generate this document" action routed through Lawyer Review (Section 7.1). All Civil Procedure Code citations require sign-off from your legal counsel before this module ships.
   171→   171→
   172→   172→#### 5.3.2 Lawyer Nomination & Engagement Letters
   173→   173→Matching logic (city, language, fee model, specialty, availability) feeding the 5.2.4 handoff packet, plus engagement-letter templates (Appendix A) with limited in-app editing and e-signature (Electronic Transactions Law compliant).
   174→   174→
   175→   175→#### 5.3.3 Regulator Dashboard (CBJ / Insurance Commission)
   176→   176→Aggregated, anonymized complaint/outcome data with k-anonymity thresholds and export tools. **Technically straightforward; commercially/legally dependent on CBJ or the Insurance Commission agreeing to receive the data and, if wanted, on an SSO integration** — a partnership prerequisite, not a build blocker for the rest of the platform.
   177→   177→
   178→   178→#### 5.3.4 Community Forum & Support Groups
   179→   179→Topic-categorized discussion, verified lawyer/NGO Q&A participation, automated + human moderation.
   180→   180→
   181→   181→### 5.4 Out of Scope / Non-Goals
   182→   182→
   183→   183→* Haqqi does not represent users in proceedings — it informs, organizes, drafts (with lawyer review), and refers.
   184→   184→* No AI-generated document is sendable/filable without passing the Lawyer Review workflow (Section 7.1), even after Phase 2 ships.
   185→   185→* No guaranteed compensation amounts or outcomes.
   186→   186→* No non-motor-accident personal injury claims in v1.
   187→   187→* No insurance underwriting or policy-sales functionality.
   188→   188→* Additional languages beyond Arabic/English are deferred, not committed to a phase.

   436→   436→### 6.4 AI/LLM Integration Layer
   437→   437→
   438→   438→* Single internal interface (e.g., `generateCompletion(prompt, context, schema)`) wrapping whichever provider is selected, so intake, drafting, and RAG-answer code never call a provider SDK directly. This is what makes the Grok-vs-Gemini decision (Section 9) a config change, not a rewrite.
   439→   439→* Structured-output mode (function calling / JSON schema) required for intake parsing and citation extraction — free-text parsing is not acceptable for data that feeds legal documents.
   440→   440→* System-prompt library stored server-side and versioned, not inlined ad hoc per call — the intake prompt, drafting prompt, and RAG-answer prompt should each be a reviewable, versioned asset (and a candidate for the same lawyer-approval gate as templates, since prompt wording affects legal-sounding output).
   441→   441→* Guardrails: confidence threshold below which the assistant must return an "uncertain, refer to lawyer" response rather than an answer; hard block on generating anything in the `court_filings` or `drafts` (CBJ/court target) tables without the case first passing through intake validation.
   442→   442→
   443→   443→### 6.5 RAG Pipeline
   444→   444→
   445→   445→1. **Ingestion**: source document → chunked (200–400 tokens, by article/clause) → embedded (768-dim) → inserted into `legal_documents` with `lawyer_verified = false`.
   446→   446→2. **Verification gate**: a legal-reviewer lawyer reviews and flips `lawyer_verified = true` via the Legal Content Management UI (Section 7.2) — the indexing/retrieval job only serves `lawyer_verified = true` rows.
   447→   447→3. **Retrieval**: hybrid BM25 + vector search, reranked to boost exact article-ID matches and topic relevance to the query.
   448→   448→4. **Answer generation**: retrieved chunks passed to the LLM layer (6.4) with instructions to cite `article_id` for every legal claim; below-threshold retrieval triggers the uncertainty fallback.
   449→   449→
   450→   450→### 6.6 Notification Layer
   451→   451→
   452→   452→* Abstracted interface (`sendNotification(channel, template, recipient, data)`) over SMS (Twilio), WhatsApp Business API, and transactional email — application code never calls a provider SDK directly, matching the pattern in 6.4.
   453→   453→* Queue-based sending with retry/backoff so a provider outage doesn't silently drop accident-deadline reminders.

   473→   473→## 7. Lawyer Review & Legal Content Management (core feature)
   474→   474→
   475→   475→Since legal counsel is already engaged for the platform, this section specifies how that oversight is actually implemented in the product — not as a risk mitigation, but as a first-class workflow with its own UI, states, and data.
   476→   476→
   477→   477→### 7.1 Per-Document Review Workflow
   478→   478→
   479→   479→**States** (on `drafts.review_status`): `pending_review → approved | rejected → sent` (sent only reachable from `approved`).
   480→   480→
   481→   481→* Any draft generated by Drafting Mode (5.2.1) enters `pending_review` automatically.
   482→   482→* A reviewing lawyer (`lawyers.is_legal_reviewer = true`) sees it in their review queue (`GET /api/lawyer/review-queue`), can edit inline, leave comments, and approve or reject.
   483→   483→* Rejection returns the draft to the user/system with `review_comments` for revision and re-submission.
   484→   484→* `sent_at` is only settable by the application when `review_status = 'approved'` — enforced in the API layer and, ideally, with a DB constraint/trigger, not just client-side logic.
   485→   485→* Every state transition is timestamped and attributed (`reviewed_by_lawyer_id`, `reviewed_at`) for the audit log required in 5.2.1.
   486→   486→
   487→   487→### 7.2 Legal Content Management (ongoing curation, not just per-document review)
   488→   488→
   489→   489→Three content types need the same lawyer-approval discipline as individual drafts, since they drive what every user sees:
   490→   490→
   491→   491→* **Compensation rules** (`legal_rules_config`): versioned; a new version is proposed, reviewed, and only becomes `is_active` after a legal reviewer approves it. The Rights Calculator and Workflow deadlines always read the currently active version.
   492→   492→* **RAG legal corpus** (`legal_documents`): nothing is retrievable by the AI layer until `lawyer_verified = true` (Section 6.5).
   493→   493→* **Document templates** (`legal_templates`): versioned MDX, same propose → approve → activate flow.
   494→   494→
   495→   495→This gives your legal counsel a real admin surface (a simple internal dashboard, not just "review a PDF over email") to keep the platform's legal content current without needing an engineer for every wording change.
   496→   496→
   497→   497→### 7.3 Roles
   498→   498→
   499→   499→* `lawyers.is_legal_reviewer` distinguishes lawyers who can approve platform content/drafts from lawyers who are simply listed in the directory (5.2.4) for case handoff — a directory lawyer isn't automatically a reviewer, and vice versa isn't required either, but your engaged legal counsel would typically be flagged `is_legal_reviewer = true`.
   500→   500→* `users.role = 'admin'` can manage lawyer verification and `is_legal_reviewer` flags.
   501→   501→
   502→   502→### 7.4 What Still Needs Legal Counsel's Direct Input
   503→   503→
   504→   504→Regardless of the review workflow above, these specific items need your lawyer's direct input before they're built into rules/templates/RAG content — the workflow enforces *that* review happens, not what the correct legal content actually is:
   505→   505→
   506→   506→* Confirmed article/regulation numbers and current deadlines (Civil Code, compulsory insurance regulation, Civil Procedure Code, CBJ instructions) — every number in Section 2 and elsewhere is a placeholder.
   507→   507→* Sign-off on the initial `legal_rules_config` v1 and each `legal_templates` entry before Phase 1/2 launch respectively.
   508→   508→* Confirmation of current CBJ complaint-channel contact details before they're hardcoded into the Complaints Directory (5.1.3).

   526→   526→## 9. Technical Validation Plan
   527→   527→
   528→   528→Concrete things worth prototyping before committing to full-scale development — this is the "validate the technical tools" work:
   529→   529→
   530→   530→1. **LLM provider bake-off**: run the same set of Arabic-language legal-intake and drafting prompts through Grok and Gemini (and any other candidate). Compare structured-output reliability, Arabic fluency/register (legal Arabic vs. plain Arabic), latency, and cost per 1K case intakes at your expected volume. Pick one and build the abstraction layer (6.4) around it — don't leave both wired in "just in case."
   531→   531→2. **RTL Arabic PDF generation spike**: confirm `@react-pdf/renderer` (or an alternative) correctly renders mixed Arabic/English, right-to-left layout, and Noto Sans Arabic embedding for a realistic complaint-letter template before building the full template library.
   532→   532→3. **Hybrid RAG retrieval accuracy**: index a small real sample of Jordanian legal text (even 20–30 lawyer-approved articles) and test whether hybrid BM25 + vector search actually surfaces the right article for representative Arabic legal queries — this is the highest-risk technical assumption in the whole spec.
   533→   533→4. **Structured intake extraction reliability**: test whether the chosen LLM reliably produces schema-valid JSON from a multi-turn Arabic conversation, including messy/incomplete user answers, before building the Calculator and Drafting Mode on top of that output.
   534→   534→5. **Notification deliverability**: confirm actual SMS/WhatsApp delivery rates and latency for Jordanian numbers through your chosen provider before relying on it for statute-of-limitations reminders.
   535→   535→6. **Review-workflow ergonomics**: build the lawyer review queue (7.1) early and have your actual engaged lawyer use it on a handful of real draft examples — this is a UI/workflow risk (will a busy lawyer actually use this queue) as much as a technical one.
